NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots

25 years of fire in Peru, on one consistent scale

NASA's two fire sensors count fires differently: VIIRS sees 4–8× more hotspots than MODIS. We harmonized both into a single, validated 2000–2026 record and use it to warn the people who protect the Amazon's Communal Reserves when a fire season is out of the ordinary.

Peru's 11 Communal Reserves

Communal Reserves are protected areas co-managed by SERNANP and the Indigenous organizations of the native communities around them. VIIRS hotspots (2012–Oct 2026) inside each reserve and its buffer zone. Click a row to see it on the map.

Why harmonize, and how

  1. Clean & grid. NASA FIRMS hotspots from MODIS (Terra, Aqua) and VIIRS (S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21) over Peru, 2000–2026: confident detections only, vegetation fires only, aggregated to a common 0.25° × month grid.
  2. One VIIRS reference. S-NPP is the reference; on days S-NPP was not observing and after it was switched off (1 Nov 2026), NOAA-20 fills in. The two agree within 1%.
  3. Learn MODIS → VIIRS. A Poisson GLM learns, cell by cell, how many hotspots VIIRS sees for a given MODIS count, month and local fire regime, in the 2012–2026 overlap. Five models were compared with leave-one-year-out validation.
  4. Reconstruct & alert. 2000–2011 are expressed as VIIRS-equivalent with a 95% band. Every month, year, cell and protected area is compared with its own history (robust z-score) to flag anomalous fire seasons.
